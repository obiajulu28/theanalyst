Capstone case studies

Each case follows the same path: context, baseline, analysis, requirements, proposed TO-BE, deliverables, then evaluation and limits. All three are capstone analyses. They contain proposals and targets, not deployed solutions or measured results.

Client Pharmacy: routine prescription delivery from 7 to 3 days

Capstone · service operations and digital workflow improvement

  1. Business context Reduce routine prescription delivery to 3 days with a safer, traceable order-to-delivery process, fewer errors and clearer ownership.
  2. Baseline Case baseline: deliveries take up to 7 days against a 3-day target; 12% of orders have capture errors; staff can see the status of 40%.
  3. Analysis Mapped the AS-IS journey from order to delivery, ran a gap analysis against the 3-day goal, and used a tailored Fishbone and 5 Whys on manual re-entry, missing validation, unclear handoffs, stock visibility and status updates.
  4. Requirements Identified stakeholders (pharmacy, patients, fulfilment, couriers, technology, customer service, compliance); prioritised requirements and user stories using MoSCoW, with acceptance criteria.
  5. Proposed TO-BE Validated order capture, shared status tracking, defined handoffs, exception alerts and patient notifications.
  6. Deliverables Gap analysis, root-cause analysis, TO-BE steps, prioritised requirements and user stories, ownership recommendations, acceptance criteria and a KPI framework.
  7. Evaluation and limits Proposed measures: order-to-delivery time (target 3 days or less), capture-error rate (baseline 12%), orders with visible status (baseline 40%), status-call volume and exception resolution time. No post-implementation improvement is claimed.

Liam Home Healthcare: care coordination and digital workflow discovery

Capstone · home-care service discovery

  1. Business context Assess how home-care coordination could be clearer for service users, care staff and coordinators.
  2. Baseline No verified baseline figures. The working problem statement is a hypothesis: fragmented scheduling, handovers, visit reporting or escalation may cause avoidable rework and limited visibility.
  3. Analysis Structured the care journey from referral and assessment to scheduling, home visits, recording and escalation, and mapped needs and handoffs between clients and families, carers, coordinators and clinical staff. Applied NHS frontline insight on communication clarity and documentation.
  4. Requirements Framed AS-IS questions, functional requirements, data and consent safeguards and acceptance tests, prioritised with MoSCoW.
  5. Proposed TO-BE Agreed visit-status definitions, documented escalation paths, structured care notes and clearer stakeholder notifications.
  6. Deliverables Stakeholder map, service-user journey map, requirements catalogue, RACI, risk and dependency register and UAT scenarios.
  7. Evaluation and limits Proposed measures: visit completion, late or missed visits, time to resolve care exceptions, record completeness and service-user feedback. No deployed solution or verified improvement exists; any prototype screens would be illustrative.

Dexcom: manufacturing backorder visibility and demand-to-supply analysis

Capstone · medtech manufacturing and supply chain

Educational case analysis. It was not commissioned by the company and does not use confidential or internal company data.

  1. Business context Investigate manufacturing scale and backorders at a medical technology business, where misaligned demand, capacity, stock and fulfilment signals can make delivery commitments uncertain.
  2. Baseline No quantitative throughput, inventory or backorder baseline was used, and no real company figures are implied.
  3. Analysis Framed order-to-production-to-shipment as a cross-functional investigation; identified the data needed (orders, backlog ageing, inventory, capacity, cycle and lead time) and working root-cause hypotheses using Fishbone and 5 Whys.
  4. Requirements Mapped stakeholders (manufacturing, planning, supply chain, customer operations, quality, digital); defined requirements and a MoSCoW-prioritised improvement backlog with acceptance criteria for reliable status and exception handling.
  5. Proposed TO-BE Consistent backlog definitions, agreed order-status ownership, stock and capacity visibility, and exception-based escalation.
  6. Deliverables Swimlane decomposition, gap analysis, stakeholder matrix, KPI design, specified sample dashboards and UAT cases for order visibility and exceptions.
  7. Evaluation and limits Suggested measures: open backorders, backlog age, on-time fulfilment, schedule adherence and production lead time. These are analytical recommendations, not implemented results.
