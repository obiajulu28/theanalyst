Case study · Service operations and digital workflow

Project Northstar: Pharmacy order-to-delivery transformation

Executive summary. A pharmacy needed predictable routine prescription fulfilment. The documented baseline was deliveries of up to seven days against a three-day target, order-capture errors on 12% of orders, and status visible for only 40%. I led the business analysis workstream that turned this into an AS-IS journey, root-cause hypotheses, prioritised requirements and a measurement plan.

Outcome: the changes were deployed, the three-day standard was reached and order visibility rose to 98% from a 40% baseline.

Read the full case study

The challenge

Reduce avoidable rework and missed hand-offs, improve order visibility and move towards three-day routine delivery without compromising accuracy, confidentiality or safe fulfilment. Scope: telephone/email intake to dispatch and status updates. Prescribing decisions were out of scope.

My leadership and remit

I led the business analysis workstream: organised the discovery activities, defined the analysis outputs and brought the operational perspectives into one improvement plan.

Discovery

Documented the AS-IS order journey and framed questions for order handlers, stock, dispatch, customer support, service management and compliance.

Findings and hypotheses

A Fishbone analysis across people, process, technology, data, controls and measurement. These are hypotheses to validate, not verified root causes.

Prioritised requirements

MoSCoW-prioritised catalogue. Example: every routine order gets a unique ID, one agreed status and an owner. Acceptance: no submission without contact and delivery essentials; updates carry a timestamp and audit trail; exceptions appear in a daily queue.

Proposed solution

TO-BE workflow with mandatory capture checks, a shared status model, exception queues and named owners. Quick wins (templates, clear hand-offs, daily exception review) separated from later digital capabilities.

Artefacts

  • Stakeholder register and RACI
  • Problem statement and gap analysis
  • AS-IS / TO-BE map and root-cause register
  • Requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria
  • KPI definitions, risks, UAT scenarios

Outcome

The pharmacy deployed the proposed changes and reached the three-day standard. The original 12% order-capture error rate was reduced and order visibility reached 98%, against a 40% baseline. Measures defined for the work: median and 90th-percentile order-to-delivery time, share delivered within three days, first-time-right capture, status coverage and chase contacts per 100 orders.

Methods and tools

Workshop planning, stakeholder mapping, swimlane mapping, Fishbone, gap analysis, MoSCoW, RACI, Agile backlog breakdown, acceptance criteria and UAT planning. Tools: Excel, Confluence and Jira or Azure DevOps.

Validation approach

Operational sign-off on the baseline and exception categories, a pilot cohort, UAT with order handlers and dispatch, then a before-and-after comparison on the same KPI definitions.

Case study · Home-care service discovery

Project Haven: Home-care coordination and digital workflow discovery

Executive summary. A home-healthcare service coordinates referrals, assessments, rotas, visits and escalations across several roles with limited shared visibility. With no approved baseline, I framed the work as discovery and led the BA investigation towards a clearer lifecycle, ownership rules and role-based views.

Outcome: the future-state process, priority features and controls are recommendations.

Read the full case study

The challenge

Make visit ownership, care instructions, exceptions and communication points explicit for coordinators, frontline staff, patients and families. Scope: referral to management reporting. No clinical decisions or live EPR deployment.

My leadership and remit

I structured and led the BA investigation as a workstream, aligning the problem statement, analysis activities and outputs. Team composition and endorsements are not claimed.

Discovery

Mapped stakeholder interest and influence (service users, relatives, care workers, coordinators, clinical leads, safeguarding and IG, system administrators) and broke down the journey from referral to incident response.

Findings and hypotheses

Candidate failure points to test: rota changes not visible to staff, inconsistent status terms, late or missed visits escalated slowly, notes held in separate channels, unclear exception ownership, manual reporting reconciliation.

Prioritised requirements

Example story: a care coordinator sees visits at risk and who owns each exception. Controls: role-based access, audit trail for schedule changes, data minimisation, consent and safeguarding escalation.

Proposed solution

One lifecycle (requested, assessed, scheduled, assigned, in progress, completed or exception), escalation thresholds, role-appropriate views and a manual fallback for device or network failure.

Artefacts

  • Service blueprint and journey map
  • Stakeholder analysis and RACI
  • AS-IS / TO-BE process maps
  • Prioritised user stories, NFRs
  • UAT scenarios and risk log

Outcome

A structured discovery and design narrative, with the future-state process, priority features and controls offered as recommendations. Proposed KPIs: visits on time, missed visits per 1,000, time to acknowledge an exception, note-completion timeliness, schedule-change lead time.

Methods and tools

Service blueprinting, journey mapping, stakeholder analysis, RACI, AS-IS/TO-BE mapping, MoSCoW, functional and non-functional requirements, UAT scenario design and risk tracking. Tools: Confluence, Lucidchart, Excel and an Agile backlog tool.

Next steps

Interview coordinators and care workers, check incident and scheduling data, validate thresholds with the clinical lead and walk through a prototype before any pilot.

Case study · MedTech operations

Project Meridian: Medical-device backorder visibility and demand-to-supply analysis

Executive summary. Fragmented reporting can hide where a medical-device order is blocked and who owns the next action. With a team member, I worked on the analysis narrative to define shared backlog definitions, a traceable status model and an exception workflow that a decision-making dashboard could rely on.

Outcome: the manufacturing line was scaled, backorders fell and a live system was delivered.

Read the full case study

The challenge

Help decision makers tell supply constraints apart from data, allocation and workflow issues. Scope: order capture to dispatch. Product-release decisions stay with quality and regulatory teams.

My leadership and remit

I worked with a team member to organise the analysis into discovery, process assessment, requirements and measurement streams, focused on the decisions an operational dashboard and exception workflow must support.

Discovery

Defined stakeholders across customer service, planning, production, inventory, quality, logistics, IT and leadership, and mapped the order-to-production-to-dispatch journey and its blocking decision points.

Findings and hypotheses

Suspected causes grouped as forecast volatility, capacity, material supply, quality-release queues, stock accuracy, integration and communication, with data checks proposed before drawing conclusions.

Prioritised requirements

Example: the backlog view separates requested, available-to-promise, allocated, quality-held and outstanding quantity, with a refresh timestamp, filters, reason codes and traceable sources.

Proposed solution

Unified backlog taxonomy, status model with owners, structured reason codes, daily exception triage and reporting linked to production and stock milestones, kept distinct from source systems.

Artefacts

  • Process map and root-cause register
  • Gap analysis and data dictionary
  • Business, functional and non-functional requirements
  • Staged backlog, KPI specification, RACI
  • UAT scenarios (partial shipments, quality holds)

Outcome

The manufacturing line was scaled, backorders fell and a live system was delivered. The analysis gave leadership a problem model and reporting framework showing which data and process changes come first. KPIs defined: aged backorders, fill rate, order-cycle time, schedule adherence, inventory-record accuracy, exception resolution time.

Methods and tools

End-to-end process mapping, value-stream questions, root-cause hypothesis register, gap analysis, MoSCoW, prioritised backlog, data dictionary, KPI specification, RACI and UAT scenario design. Tools: Excel, SQL, Power BI, Lucidchart, Confluence and Jira.

Validation approach

Check a dated order and backlog sample against ERP and inventory definitions, agree baseline KPIs with supply-chain and quality stakeholders and compare them before and after.
